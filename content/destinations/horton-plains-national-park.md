High above Nuwara Eliya, Horton Plains is a wild plateau of grassland and cloud forest more than 2,000 metres above the sea. A walking trail of about nine kilometres loops across it to World’s End, where the land drops away in a sheer cliff, and on past Baker’s Falls. Sambar deer graze in the morning mist, and the plains are part of a UNESCO World Heritage site.

On the high plateau.

**Hidden gems**

- World’s End – A sheer drop of about 870 metres, with views to the coast on a clear morning
- Mini World’s End – A smaller cliff on the same trail
- Baker’s Falls – A pretty waterfall named after the explorer Samuel Baker
- Endemic birds – Many of Sri Lanka’s highland birds live only here and in the hills around

**Best time to visit:** January to March has the clearest skies. In every season, arrive early: the clouds usually roll in by mid-morning and hide the view from World’s End.
